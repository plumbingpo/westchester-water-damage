Wet Carpet Drying in Westchester

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Westchester homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold on the Backing

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Water Held in the Pad

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Water Quality Drops

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
