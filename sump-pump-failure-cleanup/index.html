Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Westchester: What to Know

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Stored Belongings

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Outlets and Wiring

Basement outlets sit low on the wall, where rising water can reach them. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. Once the drywall is off, the plates and studs are washed, disinfected and dried.

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Carpet, padding, insulation and wet drywall that the water contacted are pulled by a crew in protective gear, bagged and removed from the property so the concrete and studs are open.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Confirm

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.

Insurance

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether the basement is finished, with drywall, carpet and trim to remove.
  • The depth of the water and the share of the basement floor under it.
  • Contents handling, meaning what is stored down there and how much can be kept.
  • The water category, which sets how much is discarded and disinfected.
