Crawl Space Water Removal: What Westchester Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Stale, mildew type air coming up around the vents in the floor
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Contaminated Water

Do not assume the water below your floor is clean. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Rot You Cannot See

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Air From Below

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.
