Attic Water Damage: What Westchester Homeowners Should Know

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. For Westchester homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. Even worse, it keeps water pressed against the framing and the ceiling drywall for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Insulation that looks matted, flattened, or darker than the insulation around it
  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Wiring

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. Do not enter the attic before someone checks the power, because wet electrical parts can shock you or start a fire.

Mold on Sheathing

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Trace

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. We protect the living space below and keep the access path contained.

3

Dry the Wood

Air movers and dehumidifiers bring the rafters, joists and sheathing back to normal moisture levels. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.
