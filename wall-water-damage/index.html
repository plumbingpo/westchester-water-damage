Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Westchester

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Warning Signs

  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
How We Fix It

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Mold Inside Cavities

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
