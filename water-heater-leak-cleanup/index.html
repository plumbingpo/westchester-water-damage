Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Westchester: What to Know

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Westchester crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.

Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.

What Happens if You Wait

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Shared Garage Wall

The wall between the garage and the house takes water into its bottom plate and drywall from the slab, and from there it reaches the floors of the living space.

Stored Belongings

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.
