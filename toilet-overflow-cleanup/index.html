Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Westchester

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

The first thing our Westchester team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • A sewer line narrowed by tree roots or a collapse, so every drain in the home runs slow.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Soaked Subfloor

Grout lines and the base of the toilet let water pass under vinyl and tile. Once wet, the plywood subfloor swells, its layers separate, and the floor covering bonded on top can come loose.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. Several inches of wall above floor level can hold moisture with no visible sign on the paint.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all pick up bacteria from bowl or drain water. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Ceiling Below

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. To learn which of these applies in your case, check the policy or phone your agent.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Category of water, as a contaminated overflow adds labor, protective measures and disinfecting.
  • Whether the toilet is upstairs and the ceiling or walls below got wet.
  • How far the water traveled, such as into a hallway, bedroom carpet or closet.
  • Any need to remove the vanity or toilet so the area under it can be cleaned and dried.
