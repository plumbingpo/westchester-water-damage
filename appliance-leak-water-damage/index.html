Appliance Leak Water Damage in Westchester: What to Know

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

On every Westchester job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Mop Up What You CanUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.
  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.
  • If it was gray discharge, which must be sanitized, or clean water from the supply.
  • The appliance involved, and if the leak was a slow seep or a fast flood.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
  • Washer supply hoses made of rubber that get weak over the years and burst from the pressure.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Adjoining Rooms

A washer hose can release water for hours. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.
