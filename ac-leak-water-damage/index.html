AC Leak Water Damage Help in Westchester

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Ceilings Under the Unit

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

Mold Near the Return

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

How We Fix It

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Stains show only part of it, so we scan the area by the air handler, the walls or ceiling beside it and the nearby flooring with a thermal camera and moisture meters.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Set Drying Equipment

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.
